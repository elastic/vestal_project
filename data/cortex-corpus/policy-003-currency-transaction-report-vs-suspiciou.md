The purpose of this section is to establish the regulatory framework and internal protocols governing the identification, documentation, and reporting of large currency transactions and potentially illicit financial activities at Cortex Bank and Trust. As a financial institution committed to safeguarding the integrity of the banking system, Cortex Bank and Trust enforces strict adherence to its internal reporting thresholds, which are designed to detect, deter, and report money laundering and terrorist financing. This policy delineates the distinct criteria and procedural requirements for Currency Transaction Reports and Suspicious Activity Reports.

A Currency Transaction Report is a mandatory filing designed to capture the physical transfer of fiat currency into or out of Cortex Bank and Trust. For the purposes of this policy, Cortex Bank and Trust defines the standard reporting threshold for a Currency Transaction Report as any single transaction involving physical currency that exceeds twelve thousand five hundred dollars. Furthermore, the bank automated transaction monitoring systems are calibrated to aggregate multiple cash transactions conducted by, or on behalf of, the same individual or entity over a rolling twenty-four-hour period. If the aggregated total of these transactions exceeds the twelve thousand five hundred dollar threshold, a Currency Transaction Report must be generated and submitted to the designated financial intelligence authority. Bank personnel are explicitly prohibited from informing customers about these specific aggregation parameters to prevent the structuring of transactions. Once a qualifying transaction or aggregated series of transactions is identified, the reporting software automatically flags the event, and the branch compliance officer must verify the customer identity and submit the finalized Currency Transaction Report within ten business days of the precipitating transaction.

Unlike the strict numerical trigger of the Currency Transaction Report, a Suspicious Activity Report relies on a combination of financial thresholds and qualitative assessments of customer behavior. Cortex Bank and Trust mandates the filing of a Suspicious Activity Report when a transaction, or a series of aggregated transactions, appears to serve no legitimate business or lawful purpose, or when the bank suspects the funds are derived from illegal activities. The internal threshold for filing a Suspicious Activity Report when a specific suspect can be identified is set at three thousand five hundred dollars. In scenarios where a suspicious transaction has occurred but the identity of the perpetrator remains unknown, the mandatory reporting threshold is elevated to seven thousand five hundred dollars. Additionally, any suspected insider abuse involving Cortex Bank and Trust employees, officers, or directors requires an immediate Suspicious Activity Report filing regardless of the dollar amount, though heightened immediate escalation to the Board of Directors is required if the insider abuse exceeds fifteen thousand dollars. Upon the detection of qualifying suspicious activity, the anti-money laundering investigations unit has a strict mandate to complete their review and file the Suspicious Activity Report within twenty-five calendar days from the initial date of detection.

It is imperative that all Cortex Bank and Trust personnel understand the operational distinction between these two reports, as well as the circumstances that necessitate dual filing. The Currency Transaction Report is purely an objective measure of physical cash volume, whereas the Suspicious Activity Report evaluates the context, intent, and nature of electronic or physical transfers. In the event that a customer conducts a physical cash transaction of fourteen thousand dollars, this will automatically trigger a Currency Transaction Report. If the teller or automated monitoring system concurrently notes that the customer sequentially deposited this amount using tightly bundled, odoriferous notes in a manner inconsistent with their stated occupation, a Suspicious Activity Report must also be filed based on the qualitative suspicion, satisfying the three thousand five hundred dollar known-suspect threshold. In such instances of dual filing, the bank staff must cross-reference the reports within the internal compliance database without referencing the existence of the Suspicious Activity Report on the Currency Transaction Report itself.

Strict confidentiality rules govern the administration of these reports. Under no circumstances may any employee of Cortex Bank and Trust disclose the filing, or the contemplation of filing, a Suspicious Activity Report to the subject of the report or any unauthorized third party. All supporting documentation, investigative notes, and final submission receipts for both Currency Transaction Reports and Suspicious Activity Reports must be retained in the bank secure archival system for a minimum of seven years from the date of filing. The Chief Compliance Officer retains ultimate oversight of these thresholds and is responsible for conducting annual reviews to ensure these internal limits remain effective at mitigating institutional risk while supporting global anti-money laundering initiatives.
