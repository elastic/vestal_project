Cortex Bank and Trust is committed to mitigating the risks of money laundering, terrorist financing, and other illicit financial activities. Central to this commitment is the rigorous identification and verification of the beneficial owners of all legal entity customers. This Anti-Money Laundering policy section establishes mandatory procedures for gathering, validating, and maintaining beneficial ownership information whenever a legal entity establishes a new relationship with Cortex Bank and Trust. This framework ensures the bank maintains a transparent understanding of the natural persons who ultimately own or control the legal entities operating within our financial ecosystem. This protocol aligns with broader regulatory mandates while imposing stricter internal safeguards to protect the institutional integrity of Cortex Bank and Trust.

For this policy, Cortex Bank and Trust defines a beneficial owner under two distinct categories: the ownership prong and the control prong. Under the ownership prong, a beneficial owner is any natural person who directly or indirectly owns eighteen percent or more of the equity interests of a legal entity customer. This eighteen percent threshold is strictly enforced across all commercial accounts, superseding less stringent industry standards. Under the control prong, a beneficial owner is a single natural person with significant responsibility to control, manage, or direct a legal entity customer. This includes a Chief Executive Officer, Managing Member, or General Partner. Every legal entity must identify at least one individual under the control prong, regardless of whether any single individual meets the eighteen percent ownership threshold.

Prior to opening any new account for a legal entity, the designated relationship manager must obtain a completed and signed Beneficial Ownership Certification Document. This document must state the names, dates of birth, residential addresses, and governmental identification numbers for every natural person meeting the criteria of either prong. For individuals with a domestic footprint, a social security number is mandatory. For foreign nationals, a passport number and country of issuance must be recorded. Cortex Bank and Trust strictly prohibits the establishment of any legal entity account if the prospective customer refuses to provide the complete certification document or attempts to provide materially incomplete information.

Upon receipt of the certification document, Cortex Bank and Trust personnel must verify the identity of each named beneficial owner. This verification process must be completed within fourteen business days of account opening. The bank employs a risk-based approach incorporating documentary and non-documentary verification methods. Documentary verification requires the procurement of unexpired, government-issued identification bearing a photograph. Non-documentary verification involves cross-referencing the provided information against specialized third-party identity verification bureaus and public records databases approved by the compliance department. If the bank cannot form a reasonable belief regarding the true identity of a beneficial owner through non-documentary means, documentary evidence becomes mandatory. Cortex Bank and Trust requires all onboarding personnel to retain secure, unalterable copies of all documentation utilized during this verification phase. These records must be maintained in the centralized compliance repository for a minimum of seven years following the formal closure of the customer relationship.

In circumstances where discrepancies arise during the verification process, the onboarding specialist must initiate a formal resolution protocol. Discrepancies may include mismatched addresses, invalid identification numbers, or adverse media findings associated with a declared beneficial owner. Minor administrative errors may be resolved through direct communication with the customer representative. Substantive anomalies must be immediately escalated to the Cortex Bank and Trust Financial Intelligence Unit. During escalation, the account will be placed in a restricted status, strictly prohibiting all outbound transactions. If the Financial Intelligence Unit determines that the identity cannot be verified within thirty days, the account must be closed, and the compliance officer will evaluate the circumstances for potential suspicious activity report filing.

The identification of beneficial ownership is not exclusively an onboarding requirement. Cortex Bank and Trust mandates the ongoing maintenance and periodic refreshing of beneficial ownership information throughout the customer relationship lifecycle. Legal entity customers classified as high risk must submit an updated certification document every twelve months. Standard risk entities are subject to a refresh cycle of thirty-six months. Furthermore, an immediate review is triggered by anomalous events, such as a significant change in transaction volumes, modifications to authorized signers, or notification of an acquisition. Confirmed changes to the beneficial ownership structure must be documented and verified in accordance with standard onboarding protocols to ensure continuous regulatory compliance.
