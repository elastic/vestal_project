The purpose of this section is to establish the formal regulatory escalation procedures for Cortex Bank and Trust regarding all matters related to anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing. Cortex Bank and Trust requires all employees, contractors, and automated transaction monitoring systems to adhere strictly to the tiered escalation framework detailed herein. This framework ensures that suspicious activities are identified, investigated, documented, and reported to the appropriate regulatory authorities in a timely and compliant manner. The foundation of this process relies on clear communication channels between front-line personnel, the Financial Intelligence Unit, senior management, and external regulatory bodies.

Phase one of the escalation procedure involves initial detection and alert generation. Cortex Bank and Trust employs automated transaction monitoring software calibrated to generate alerts based on specific internal thresholds. For example, cash transactions or structured cash deposits aggregating to fifteen thousand dollars or more within a rolling five-day period will automatically trigger a Tier One alert. Similarly, international wire transfers exceeding forty two thousand five hundred dollars, especially those originating from or destined for high-risk jurisdictions, require immediate system escalation. Front-line staff must also manually escalate any behavioral anomalies, such as a customer refusing to provide standard identification documentation, directly to the branch compliance officer within two business days of the encounter.

Upon generation of a Tier One alert or a manual referral, the matter enters phase two, which is the initial review by the Financial Intelligence Unit of Cortex Bank and Trust. The designated investigator has a maximum of fourteen calendar days to review the alert, gather necessary customer due diligence documentation, and analyze the transaction history. During this investigative phase, the Financial Intelligence Unit is strictly prohibited from contacting the customer or their associates regarding the nature of the inquiry, as doing so constitutes a severe violation of anti-tipping-off regulations. Investigators must rely exclusively on internal bank records, publicly available information, and secure information-sharing requests made through approved regulatory channels. Any suspected breach of confidentiality during this phase must be immediately reported to the internal audit division. If the investigator determines the activity lacks a reasonable economic or lawful purpose, the alert is upgraded to a formalized case. The investigator must document all findings in the centralized case management system. If the case involves transaction volumes exceeding one hundred and fifty thousand dollars, or if the subject is identified as a politically exposed person, the case must be escalated immediately to the Deputy Chief Compliance Officer for expedited review.

Phase three dictates the procedures for management review and the formal committee evaluation. Cases that the Financial Intelligence Unit recommends for regulatory reporting are presented to the Suspicious Activity Report Committee, which convenes bi-weekly. This committee comprises the Chief Compliance Officer, the Head of Fraud, and senior legal counsel for Cortex Bank and Trust. The committee is responsible for making the final determination on whether a formal filing is legally required under applicable statutes. For any case involving potential terrorist financing, human trafficking, or suspected fraud exceeding five hundred thousand dollars, the committee must convene an emergency ad-hoc session within forty-eight hours of the case elevation. The committee also reviews a randomized ten percent sample of cases where the Financial Intelligence Unit recommended against filing a report. This secondary quality assurance mechanism ensures that Cortex Bank and Trust is not systematically under-reporting potential money laundering activities. If the committee overturns a non-filing recommendation, the primary investigator must undergo mandatory retraining on identifying red flags within the commercial banking sector.

Phase four encompasses the final regulatory reporting and board notification protocols. If the committee approves a filing, the Anti-Money Laundering Officer of Cortex Bank and Trust must submit the report to the appropriate financial intelligence authority within thirty calendar days from the date the initial suspicious activity was deemed reportable. The Anti-Money Laundering Officer is solely responsible for verifying that the narrative section of the report is comprehensive, factually accurate, and devoid of any speculative assertions. In scenarios where the identity of the suspect is unknown, this reporting window may be extended to sixty days. Furthermore, any investigation resulting in a filed report involving amounts greater than two million dollars, or implicating a Cortex Bank and Trust employee in collusive activities, mandates a direct and immediate briefing to the Board of Directors Risk Committee.

In the event that an investigation uncovers activity that poses an immediate threat to national security, or involves an ongoing money laundering scheme requiring immediate intervention, Cortex Bank and Trust will bypass standard internal timelines. The Chief Compliance Officer is authorized to contact federal law enforcement agencies directly via the expedited telephonic reporting procedures. All documentation, working papers, decisions not to file, and submitted reports generated through this escalation process must be retained in a secure, immutable digital archive for a minimum of eight years from the date of the final decision. This ensures that Cortex Bank and Trust maintains complete historical integrity for subsequent regulatory examinations and internal compliance audits.
