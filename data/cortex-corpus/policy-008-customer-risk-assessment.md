The Customer Risk Assessment policy at Cortex Bank and Trust establishes the framework for identifying, measuring, and mitigating money laundering and terrorist financing risks associated with our client base. This comprehensive assessment process ensures that the bank allocates its compliance resources efficiently, applying enhanced scrutiny to relationships that pose a heightened threat to the institution. Every individual and entity seeking to establish a relationship with Cortex Bank and Trust must undergo a standardized risk evaluation prior to account opening and continuously throughout the lifecycle of the relationship.

Cortex Bank and Trust employs a proprietary automated risk scoring matrix to evaluate customer risk. This quantitative and qualitative methodology analyzes four primary risk dimensions: customer type, geographic location, products and services utilized, and the delivery channels through which the customer interacts with the bank. Customer type risk evaluates the inherent threat posed by the client's occupation, industry, or legal structure. Geographic risk assesses the money laundering vulnerability of the jurisdictions where the customer resides, operates, or conducts significant transactional activity. Product and service risk focuses on the features of the bank offerings the customer intends to use, particularly those enabling rapid, cross-border, or anonymous movement of funds. Delivery channel risk examines how the customer accesses the bank, placing higher risk on non-face-to-face interactions and intermediary-driven relationships.

The automated matrix assigns numerical values to each variable within these four dimensions, generating an aggregate Customer Risk Score ranging from zero to one thousand. A base score is calculated during the initial onboarding phase using data collected through the Customer Identification Program. Cortex Bank and Trust classifies customers into three distinct risk tiers based on these numerical thresholds. Customers receiving a score between zero and three hundred forty-nine are designated as Low Risk. Those scoring between three hundred fifty and six hundred ninety-nine are classified as Medium Risk. Any customer receiving a score of seven hundred or above is automatically categorized as High Risk. Furthermore, the presence of specific critical risk factors, such as identification as a Politically Exposed Person or operating as an intensive cash-based business, will bypass the numerical scoring matrix and mandate an immediate High Risk designation regardless of the aggregate score.

The assigned risk tier dictates the level of ongoing due diligence and transactional monitoring applied to the account. Low and Medium Risk customers are subject to Standard Customer Due Diligence procedures. High Risk customers are subjected to Enhanced Due Diligence, which requires the collection of corroborating documentation regarding the customer's source of wealth, source of funds, and the anticipated volume and velocity of transactions. Furthermore, the establishment of any High Risk relationship requires the explicit written approval of the designated Regional Compliance Officer or the Chief Anti-Money Laundering Officer, depending on the specific risk vectors identified.

Cortex Bank and Trust treats the Customer Risk Assessment as a dynamic process rather than a static, one-time evaluation. The bank mandates periodic reviews of all customer risk profiles to ensure the assigned rating remains reflective of the customer's actual behavior. High Risk profiles must be subjected to a comprehensive manual review every twelve months. Medium Risk profiles require re-evaluation every twenty-four months, while Low Risk profiles are reassessed every thirty-six months. In addition to these scheduled periodic reviews, Cortex Bank and Trust implements event-driven reviews. The automated transaction monitoring system is calibrated to detect material deviations from a customer's anticipated activity profile. Trigger events, such as a sudden increase in international wire transfers exceeding fifty thousand dollars in a single business week, adverse media findings, or a change in beneficial ownership, will immediately prompt an ad hoc risk reassessment.

The front-line business units bear the initial responsibility for collecting accurate and complete data to feed the risk assessment matrix during onboarding. However, the Financial Intelligence Unit maintains ultimate authority over the final risk rating and possesses the unilateral ability to manually override a system-generated score if qualitative factors suggest the automated assessment does not adequately capture the true risk of the relationship. Any manual override resulting in a risk downgrade must be accompanied by a detailed written justification and approved by the Chief Anti-Money Laundering Officer. All data, scoring rationales, and review schedules associated with the Customer Risk Assessment must be retained in the bank's centralized compliance repository for a period of no less than seven years following the termination of the customer relationship.
