The purpose of this section is to establish the standards and protocols employed by Cortex Bank and Trust to conduct ongoing transaction monitoring. As a regulated financial institution, Cortex Bank and Trust is strictly obligated to detect, investigate, and report suspicious activities indicative of money laundering and other illicit financial behaviors. Ongoing transaction monitoring represents a critical pillar of the broader compliance framework. The Bank utilizes a hybrid approach comprising automated surveillance systems and manual human review to scrutinize customer transactions, ensuring that account activity remains consistent with the known business profile, risk rating, and expected historical behavior of the client.

Cortex Bank and Trust deploys an advanced automated transaction monitoring system to review daily account activities across all business lines. The system flags transactions that deviate from established baselines or breach predefined quantitative thresholds. To mitigate the risk of structuring, the system generates an alert when a customer conducts multiple cash deposits aggregating to eight thousand five hundred dollars or more within a rolling three-day period. For international transfers, an immediate compliance review alert is triggered for any outbound transfer exceeding forty-two thousand dollars that involves a high-risk jurisdiction. Furthermore, the system employs velocity monitoring, flagging any account exhibiting a transfer volume exceeding one hundred and fifty thousand dollars within a single business week if such volume represents a deviation of more than forty percent from the rolling ninety-day average of the account.

When the automated surveillance system identifies activity breaching these parameters, it generates an exception alert routed directly to the Cortex Bank and Trust Financial Intelligence Unit. Alerts must be assigned to a Level One compliance analyst within one business day. The assigned analyst conducts a preliminary investigation to determine if the flagged activity has a plausible economic rationale. This review encompasses an examination of the customer profile, historical transaction records, and source of wealth declarations. The analyst must document their findings meticulously in the case management system. If the analyst concludes that the transaction aligns with the profile of the customer and poses no substantive risk, the alert may be closed as a false positive, provided that a detailed justification is recorded.

Should the analyst be unable to establish a legitimate economic rationale, the alert must be escalated to a Level Two senior investigator within five business days. The Level Two investigator at Cortex Bank and Trust is tasked with performing a comprehensive forensic review. This entails submitting requests for information to the customer relationship manager to obtain documentary evidence, such as invoices or contracts, to substantiate the transaction. If the customer fails to provide satisfactory documentation within ten business days, or if the provided information further elevates the suspicion, the investigator will prepare a comprehensive investigative summary recommending the filing of a Suspicious Activity Report.

All recommendations for a Suspicious Activity Report must be submitted to the Cortex Bank and Trust Suspicious Activity Report Committee for final adjudication. The Committee convenes bi-weekly to review investigative summaries. If the Committee votes to proceed, the designated compliance officer will file the report with the appropriate financial intelligence authority within thirty calendar days from the initial date of suspicion detection. Concurrently, the Committee determines the necessary risk mitigation actions regarding the customer relationship. Depending on the severity of the suspicious activity, Cortex Bank and Trust may impose enhanced due diligence measures, restrict specific account functionalities, or initiate the complete closure of the customer accounts.

To maintain the efficacy of the ongoing transaction monitoring program, Cortex Bank and Trust mandates rigorous periodic reviews of its automated alert thresholds. The Bank conducts an independent validation of the transaction monitoring system every eighteen months to ensure the algorithms function precisely as designed and remain calibrated to the current risk appetite. Additionally, the compliance technology team performs quarterly testing to evaluate the ratio of false positives to productive alerts, allowing the Bank to adjust its threshold parameters continuously. Any modification to the transaction monitoring rules or quantitative thresholds requires the documented approval of the Chief Compliance Officer and must be preserved in the permanent audit logs.
