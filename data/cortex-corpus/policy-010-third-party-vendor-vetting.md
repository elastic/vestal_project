The purpose of this section is to establish the framework utilized by Cortex Bank and Trust to mitigate anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing risks associated with third-party vendors, suppliers, and service providers. Cortex Bank and Trust recognizes that vulnerabilities within a third-party supply chain can expose the institution to significant regulatory, reputational, and financial risks. Therefore, Cortex Bank and Trust mandates a comprehensive, risk-based vetting process for all prospective and existing third-party relationships to ensure full compliance with internal risk tolerances and external regulatory expectations.

Every prospective vendor must undergo a standardized risk assessment prior to the execution of any formal contract or the disbursement of any funds. The Vendor Risk Management department, in conjunction with the Financial Crimes Compliance unit, is responsible for categorizing each vendor into one of three risk tiers, which are Low, Moderate, or High. This categorization is driven by the nature of the service provided, the geographic location of the vendor, and the vendor's level of access to Cortex Bank and Trust customer data or financial infrastructure.

For all vendor engagements with an annualized contract value exceeding fifty thousand dollars, or for any vendor categorized as Moderate or High risk regardless of contract value, Cortex Bank and Trust requires enhanced due diligence. The prospective vendor must submit a comprehensive corporate profile, including certificates of incorporation, proof of regulatory standing in their home jurisdiction, and detailed anti-money laundering policy documentation if the vendor operates within the financial services sector. Furthermore, Cortex Bank and Trust strictly enforces a beneficial ownership threshold for these entities. Prospective vendors must identify any individual or entity holding a fifteen percent or greater equity stake or voting interest in the company. 

Once the vendor provides the required documentation, the Financial Crimes Compliance unit conducts rigorous screening against global sanctions lists, politically exposed persons databases, and adverse media repositories. If any beneficial owner or key executive generates a true match on a restricted list, the onboarding process is immediately suspended. A formal escalation is then routed to the Chief Compliance Officer of Cortex Bank and Trust, who retains the ultimate authority to approve or reject the relationship based on a comprehensive risk narrative. Exceptions to the fifteen percent beneficial ownership rule are exceptionally rare and require unanimous approval from the Executive Risk Committee.

Cortex Bank and Trust employs a continuous monitoring approach rather than treating vendor vetting as an isolated onboarding event. Vendors classified as High risk, such as those providing international payment processing or those domiciled in jurisdictions with a Corruptions Perceptions Index score below forty, are subject to a mandatory, comprehensive file refresh every one hundred and eighty days. Moderate risk vendors undergo this refresh cycle every fourteen months, while Low risk vendors are reviewed every thirty-six months. In addition to these periodic reviews, the names of all approved vendors and their declared beneficial owners are fed into the automated nightly screening engine of Cortex Bank and Trust.

Should the continuous monitoring system flag a newly designated sanction or a severe adverse media event related to an active vendor, the system automatically generates a priority alert. The Financial Crimes Compliance unit must adjudicate these alerts within forty-eight hours of generation. If the alert is validated as a material breach of anti-money laundering regulations or internal risk appetite, Cortex Bank and Trust will initiate the vendor termination protocol. The business line managing the vendor relationship is granted a maximum of forty-five days to offboard the vendor and transition services, during which time all financial disbursements to the vendor are placed in a holding account pending regulatory reporting and clearance.

To ensure the integrity of these procedures, Cortex Bank and Trust conducts an annual independent audit of the third-party vendor vetting program. The Internal Audit department selects a randomized five percent sample of all vendor files onboarded within the preceding twelve months to verify that risk scores were accurately calculated, beneficial ownership documentation was fully verified, and screening protocols were executed without manual bypass. Any systemic deficiencies identified during this audit must be remediated by the Vendor Risk Management department within ninety days, with regular progress updates submitted directly to the Board of Directors of Cortex Bank and Trust.
