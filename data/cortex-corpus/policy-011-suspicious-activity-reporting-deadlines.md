The following section outlines the definitive regulatory and internal protocols for Cortex Bank and Trust regarding the mandatory timelines for submitting Suspicious Activity Reports to the applicable financial authorities. Adherence to these deadlines is a strict legal obligation. Cortex Bank and Trust requires all personnel to execute their duties with promptness to ensure no filing is delayed beyond the statutory limits established within this policy. The primary objective is to equip law enforcement with timely intelligence to disrupt illicit financial networks.

The statutory reporting clock commences on the Date of Initial Detection. For Cortex Bank and Trust compliance protocols, the Date of Initial Detection is formally defined as the exact date an authorized investigator within the Financial Crimes Compliance Unit reaches a preliminary conclusion that a transaction meets the criteria for suspicion. This date does not necessarily correspond to the date the automated transaction monitoring systems generated an alert, nor does it match the transaction date. However, Cortex Bank and Trust mandates that all automated alerts be reviewed, and a determination of suspicion be made, no later than fourteen calendar days from system generation. Unjustified delays in reviewing alerts to artificially postpone the Date of Initial Detection are strictly prohibited.

Once the Date of Initial Detection is established, Cortex Bank and Trust requires that a standard Suspicious Activity Report be fully drafted, approved by the designated Compliance Officer, and electronically transmitted within twenty-five calendar days. This twenty-five-day deadline applies unconditionally whenever a suspect can be definitively identified. To ensure optimal resource allocation, our internal matrix dictates expedited internal processing for specific thresholds. Any suspected structuring of cash deposits aggregating to twelve thousand five hundred dollars or more within a five-day period must be processed through the internal approval queue within ten days. Similarly, suspicions involving complex corporate shell networks with potential exposure exceeding thirty-five thousand dollars mandate immediate escalation to the Senior Director of AML Operations to ensure the twenty-five-day deadline is comfortably met.

In scenarios where Cortex Bank and Trust investigators recognize suspicious activity but are unable to identify a specific suspect despite exhaustive internal reviews, the reporting deadline is conditionally extended. Under these circumstances, the compliance unit is granted an additional thirty days, extending the maximum allowable filing period to fifty-five calendar days from the Date of Initial Detection. This extension is exclusively designed to allow personnel adequate time to consult external databases and attempt to unearth the identity of the transacting parties. If, at the conclusion of this fifty-five-day period, the suspect remains unidentified, the report must be filed immediately detailing the activity and explicitly stating that the subject remains unknown.

Conversely, critical scenarios demand accelerated reporting timelines that supersede the standard twenty-five-day rule. If an investigator at Cortex Bank and Trust uncovers activity suggesting ongoing terrorist financing or violent crimes, standard filing timelines are immediately suspended. In such events, the investigator must notify the Chief Compliance Officer immediately. Protocol requires telephonic notification to federal law enforcement within twenty-four hours of detection. Following this verbal notification, a comprehensive Suspicious Activity Report must be formalized and filed within ten calendar days. Furthermore, cyber-enabled financial crimes resulting in an unrecoverable loss exceeding seventy-five thousand dollars trigger an expedited fifteen-day filing requirement to maximize asset recovery potential.

To guarantee compliance with these stringent deadlines, Cortex Bank and Trust utilizes an automated tracking architecture. This system applies a hard-lock protocol preventing any further edits to a report draft forty-eight hours prior to its final deadline, compelling final review. All dates of detection, investigative notes, and deadline extensions must be immutably recorded. These records, along with the filed reports and all supporting evidentiary documentation, must be retained in an encrypted format for a minimum of seven years from the date of final submission.
