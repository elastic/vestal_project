Cortex Bank and Trust establishes this Audit Trail and Documentation policy to ensure all Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing efforts are meticulously recorded, securely stored, and readily retrievable. This policy complies with federal and international mandates requiring financial institutions to maintain comprehensive records that enable the reconstruction of individual transactions and provide a clear history of customer due diligence. The meticulous preservation of these records is vital to protecting the institution from illicit financial activities and facilitating investigations by law enforcement agencies and regulatory bodies.

The documentation requirements extend across all departments and apply to all customer interactions and financial activities conducted through Cortex Bank and Trust. Required records encompass a broad spectrum of customer data, including signature cards, corporate resolutions, customer identification program documents, beneficial ownership registers, and enhanced due diligence reports. Furthermore, a complete audit trail of all electronic and paper-based transactions, including wire transfers, automated clearing house payments, and internal transfers, must be preserved. Suspicious Activity Reports, along with all supporting documentation and internal alert disposition logs, constitute a critical component of this retention scope. All records must capture the precise timestamp, the identity of the personnel executing the transaction or review, and the regulatory rationale for any manual interventions or overrides.

Cortex Bank and Trust mandates a strict minimum retention period of eight years following the closure of an account or the completion of a standalone transaction. For accounts designated as high-risk under the internal risk scoring matrix, including politically exposed persons and non-resident corporate entities, the retention period is extended to twelve years. The Bank requires specialized audit trail logging for all cross-border wire transfers exceeding the internal threshold of fourteen thousand five hundred dollars. Whenever a transaction breaches this specific monetary threshold, the automated monitoring system generates a comprehensive dossier that includes the origin of funds, intermediate routing data, and final beneficiary information. Bank personnel must append manual review notes to these dossiers within three business days, detailing the verified economic purpose of the transaction and validating the source of funds.

The technological infrastructure at Cortex Bank and Trust is designed to support an immutable and tamper-evident audit trail. The core banking platform interfaces directly with the central compliance repository, ensuring that once a financial or investigative record is created, it cannot be altered or deleted prior to the expiration of its mandated retention period. Any authorized amendments to customer data must generate a distinct version history log, capturing the pre-alteration state, the post-alteration state, the user identification number of the employee making the change, and the approving supervisor. In the event of a regulatory inquiry or law enforcement subpoena, Cortex Bank and Trust is obligated to retrieve and produce the requested documentation within forty-eight hours. To guarantee this expedited response time, the compliance technology division must conduct quarterly retrieval drills, randomly selecting fifty historical records spanning various asset classes and origination dates to verify system efficiency.

The Chief Anti-Money Laundering Officer is ultimately responsible for the integrity of the audit trail. The internal audit division must execute biannual reviews of the documentation framework, testing the completeness of customer due diligence files and the accuracy of transaction logs. A secondary operational threshold dictates that any transaction alert disposition taking longer than fourteen days to resolve must trigger an automatic escalation report to the Board Audit Committee. This report must document the root cause of the delay, the specific personnel involved in the bottleneck, and the interim risk mitigation steps applied to the customer account. Such rigorous oversight ensures that the narrative of every customer relationship remains coherent and entirely transparent to independent examiners.

Upon the expiration of the applicable retention periods, Cortex Bank and Trust enforces strict data sanitization and physical destruction protocols to protect customer privacy and manage risk. Electronic records must be purged using military-grade cryptographic erasure techniques that render the data completely unrecoverable across all primary and backup servers. Physical documents must be shredded to a micro-cut standard on site before being transferred to a certified disposal vendor. The destruction process itself requires its own audit trail. A formal certificate of destruction must be generated for every purge event, countersigned by the compliance records manager, and retained permanently to demonstrate that the Bank adhered strictly to its information lifecycle management duties.
