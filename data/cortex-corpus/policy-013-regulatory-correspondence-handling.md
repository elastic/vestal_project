The purpose of this section is to establish the overarching framework within Cortex Bank and Trust for the intake, review, routing, and resolution of all correspondence originating from regulatory authorities, law enforcement agencies, and other governing bodies concerning Anti-Money Laundering compliance matters. Cortex Bank and Trust recognizes that prompt, accurate communication with regulatory bodies is a fundamental pillar of our compliance posture. This policy delineates the responsibilities of the Compliance Department, the Legal Department, and the Board of Directors in managing such communications, ensuring all regulatory inquiries and informal requests are handled with the utmost diligence.

For the purposes of this policy, regulatory correspondence encompasses any written or formal verbal communication directed to Cortex Bank and Trust by state or federal regulatory agencies and law enforcement task forces. This includes grand jury subpoenas, targeted examination letters, notices of apparent liability, and routine supervisory requests. It is the strict mandate of Cortex Bank and Trust that no employee outside of the designated regulatory liaison team is permitted to respond independently to any regulatory inquiry concerning Anti-Money Laundering matters. 

Upon receipt of regulatory correspondence at any branch or corporate office of Cortex Bank and Trust, the receiving employee must immediately forward the communication to the Central Regulatory Intake Desk without taking further action. The Intake Desk is required to log the correspondence into the Enterprise Compliance Tracking System within four operational hours of initial receipt. The logging process must capture the date of receipt, the originating agency, the specific nature of the inquiry, and the assigned priority tier. The Chief Bank Secrecy Act Officer must be notified of all incoming correspondence no later than the close of business on the logging date.

Cortex Bank and Trust employs a tiered classification system to govern response timelines and escalation protocols, utilizing strictly internal thresholds to ensure promptness. Tier One communications, which include subpoenas, enforcement action notices, and communications indicating imminent legal risk, mandate a preliminary internal assessment within twelve hours of receipt. A formal acknowledgement of receipt must be dispatched to the originating agency within forty-eight hours, and a final, fully vetted response must be compiled and submitted within fourteen calendar days, unless the agency specifies a more restrictive deadline. Tier Two communications, comprising routine examination requests and general supervisory inquiries, require an internal assessment within three business days and a comprehensive response within twenty-eight calendar days. Tier Three communications, which involve broad informational requests or industry-wide surveys, are allocated a response window of up to forty-five calendar days.

The formulation of responses requires meticulous coordination across divisions of Cortex Bank and Trust. The Chief Bank Secrecy Act Officer acts as the primary author and coordinator for all Anti-Money Laundering regulatory responses. Prior to the dissemination of any written response, the draft must undergo a rigorous review process. Responses to Tier One communications require dual approval signatures from the Chief Compliance Officer and the General Counsel of Cortex Bank and Trust. Furthermore, any correspondence detailing a systemic failure, a breach of internal monetary thresholds exceeding five hundred thousand dollars in unmonitored transaction volume, or involving potential criminal liability for the institution must be escalated to the Risk and Audit Committee of the Board of Directors. The Committee must receive a comprehensive briefing within seventy-two hours of the escalation trigger.

Confidentiality and the strict control of information are paramount in the handling of regulatory correspondence. Cortex Bank and Trust strictly prohibits the unauthorized internal dissemination of regulatory inquiries, particularly those related to suspicious activity reporting or ongoing law enforcement investigations, to prevent any potential tipping off or obstruction of justice. Employees involved in the drafting or review of regulatory responses are bound by enhanced non-disclosure protocols and must operate within secure, compartmentalized digital environments when accessing related case files.

Finally, Cortex Bank and Trust is obligated to maintain a pristine and comprehensive audit trail of all regulatory interactions. All original inbound correspondence, internal drafts, evidentiary attachments, and final outbound responses must be preserved within the secure, immutable vault of the Enterprise Compliance Tracking System for a minimum retention period of ten years from the date of the final case closure. The internal audit division shall conduct semi-annual independent reviews of the regulatory correspondence handling process to ensure strict adherence to these established timelines, thresholds, and quality control measures, reporting their findings directly to the Board of Directors.
