Cortex Bank and Trust is committed to mitigating the risks associated with money laundering and terrorist financing. Wire transfers, due to their speed, finality, and global reach, represent a high-risk transaction method requiring stringent oversight. This section outlines the bank policies regarding the identification, escalation, and reporting of suspicious wire transfer activities. All Cortex Bank and Trust personnel involved in the processing, approval, or review of wire transfers must remain vigilant in identifying activities that deviate from a customer established transactional profile.

To establish a baseline for enhanced due diligence, Cortex Bank and Trust has implemented specific automated and manual review thresholds. Any outgoing or incoming international wire transfer exceeding twenty-four thousand five hundred dollars, or its foreign currency equivalent, requires an automatic secondary review by the central compliance unit. For domestic transfers, the threshold is set at thirty-seven thousand dollars. Furthermore, a velocity threshold is triggered if a single account initiates or receives more than five wire transfers within a contiguous forty-eight hour period, regardless of the individual monetary values. Transactions meeting these thresholds are not inherently illicit, but they mandate the completion of a Wire Transfer Enhanced Due Diligence Form before the funds are released or credited to the receiving account.

Personnel must evaluate the nature and context of wire transfers against known behavioral red flags. A primary indicator of potential illicit activity is a wire transfer that lacks a clear, legitimate economic or business purpose. For example, personnel should scrutinize transactions where a personal retail account suddenly begins initiating high-value commercial wires, or conversely, when a corporate entity routes funds to anomalous personal accounts. Another significant red flag involves structuring attempts, where a customer requests multiple wire transfers just below the aforementioned secondary review thresholds, such as initiating three domestic transfers of thirty-six thousand dollars on consecutive days to avoid triggering central compliance unit oversight.

Geographic anomalies are critical components of wire transfer risk assessment at Cortex Bank and Trust. Heightened scrutiny must be applied when funds are directed to or received from jurisdictions known for stringent bank secrecy laws, high levels of corruption, or inadequate anti-money laundering regulations. Personnel must also identify instances where a customer frequently wires funds to high-risk geographic locations that bear no logical relationship to the customer known business operations, supply chain, or personal background. Additionally, repeated transfers to unverified beneficiaries, or to shell companies lacking a discernible physical footprint, must immediately trigger an enhanced investigation.

Upon the identification of any wire transfer red flag, or if a transaction breaches the designated velocity and volume thresholds under suspicious circumstances, Cortex Bank and Trust employees must follow a strict escalation procedure. First, the employee must place an administrative hold on the wire transfer, preventing the dispersal of funds. Second, the employee must document the specific nature of the anomaly in the internal transaction monitoring system. Third, the matter must be escalated immediately to the Bank Secrecy Act Officer or a designated compliance manager. Employees are strictly prohibited from informing the customer that their transaction is under review for suspicious activity, as doing so constitutes tipping off, which is a severe violation of federal law and bank policy.

The Bank Secrecy Act Officer will conduct a comprehensive review of the escalated transaction, analyzing the customer historical account activity, onboarding documentation, and the stated purpose of the wire transfer. If the compliance unit determines that the transaction lacks a reasonable explanation or poses an unacceptable risk, Cortex Bank and Trust will reject the outgoing wire or freeze the incoming funds in accordance with applicable regulatory guidelines. Subsequently, the Bank Secrecy Act Officer will prepare and file a Suspicious Activity Report within the federally mandated timeframe of thirty days from the initial detection of the suspicious behavior.

Maintaining the integrity of Cortex Bank and Trust wire transfer operations requires continuous education and rigorous auditing. All wire room staff, relationship managers, and compliance analysts are required to complete specialized wire transfer red flag training on a semiannual basis. Furthermore, the internal audit department will conduct quarterly retrospective reviews of wire transfer logs to ensure that all threshold-based holds and escalated investigations were handled in strict adherence to this policy. Failure to comply with these monitoring and reporting procedures may result in disciplinary action up to and including termination of employment.
