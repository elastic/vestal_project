Cortex Bank and Trust recognizes that trade-based money laundering represents a significant vulnerability within the global financial system and poses a material risk to our institution's trade finance operations. Trade-based money laundering involves the exploitation of international trade transactions to obscure the illicit origins of funds, transferring value through the misrepresentation of price, quantity, or quality of goods and services. To mitigate these risks, Cortex Bank and Trust has established this policy section to guide all personnel within the Global Trade Finance Division, Corporate Banking, and the Financial Intelligence Unit in identifying, escalating, and mitigating suspicious activities associated with trade-based money laundering. All employees engaged in processing, approving, or reviewing trade finance transactions, including letters of credit, documentary collections, and open account trade financing, are required to rigorously apply the indicators and procedures detailed herein.

The primary mechanism for trade-based money laundering involves the manipulation of invoicing and trade documentation. Cortex Bank and Trust personnel must remain vigilant for material discrepancies between the stated value of goods on commercial invoices and the fair market value of those identical goods. An indicator of significant concern arises when goods are invoiced at prices substantially above or below prevailing market rates, thereby facilitating the transfer of excess value between the buyer and seller. Personnel must also scrutinize documentation for inconsistencies regarding the quantity or description of the goods. Suspicion is warranted if the description of goods on a bill of lading does not match the commercial invoice, or if the size or weight of the shipment is fundamentally incompatible with the described commodity. Furthermore, the submission of altered, fabricated, or illegible documentation, particularly when the customer is unwilling or unable to provide original or certified copies upon request by Cortex Bank and Trust, constitutes a severe red flag requiring immediate escalation.

Transacting entities and the routing of goods provide critical context for identifying illicit activity. Cortex Bank and Trust employees must thoroughly evaluate the nature of the business of all parties involved in a trade transaction. A key indicator is the involvement of trading entities whose stated line of business is entirely inconsistent with the commodities being traded, such as a software development firm importing large quantities of raw agricultural materials. Additionally, personnel must closely monitor transactions involving complex or illogical shipping routes, particularly those where goods transit through high-risk jurisdictions or free trade zones without a clear economic or logistical justification. The utilization of shell companies, characterized by a lack of physical presence, minimal registered capital, or an inability to identify ultimate beneficial owners, further elevates the risk profile. Transactions involving counterparties located in jurisdictions subject to comprehensive sanctions or designated as high-risk by global regulatory bodies require enhanced due diligence and mandatory secondary review by the Trade Compliance Department.

To ensure the systematic detection of anomalous trade activities, Cortex Bank and Trust utilizes a risk-based threshold screening methodology. Any trade finance transaction or series of linked transactions within a consecutive thirty-day period exceeding a combined value of two million four hundred and fifty thousand dollars requires an automated secondary valuation check against proprietary market pricing databases. Furthermore, if the variance between the declared invoice value and the median market database valuation exceeds fifteen percent, the transaction will be automatically suspended and routed to the Trade Finance Compliance Unit for manual review. For transactions involving open account trade where Cortex Bank and Trust only processes the payment, any cross-border wire transfer exceeding four hundred and seventy-five thousand dollars that contains trade-related reference data in the payment narrative must undergo automated screening for high-risk port cities and flagged logistical providers. Staff must complete their manual review of these systemic alerts within four business days of generation.

Upon identifying any combination of trade-based money laundering indicators or upon the failure of a transaction to satisfy the threshold variance reviews, Cortex Bank and Trust personnel must immediately secure the transaction and submit an internal suspicious activity referral to the Financial Intelligence Unit. Personnel are strictly prohibited from executing the transaction, alerting the customer to the investigation, or requesting additional clarifying documents from the client once an internal referral has been submitted, to avoid the risk of tipping off the subject. The Financial Intelligence Unit holds the exclusive authority to determine whether the identified anomalies warrant the filing of a Suspicious Activity Report with the appropriate regulatory authorities. All documentation related to the transaction, the internal referral, and the subsequent investigation must be retained in the secure compliance archive for a minimum of seven years following the closure of the account or the execution of the transaction. Mandatory training on these specific trade-based money laundering indicators is required annually for all front-line trade finance and compliance staff.
