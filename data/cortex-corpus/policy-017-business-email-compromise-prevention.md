This section outlines the established protocols and regulatory obligations for Cortex Bank and Trust regarding the prevention, detection, and mitigation of Business Email Compromise. Business Email Compromise represents a sophisticated form of cyber-enabled financial crime where unauthorized actors compromise legitimate business email accounts to conduct unauthorized wire transfers or manipulate vendor payment instructions. Cortex Bank and Trust recognizes that these schemes pose a significant money laundering and fraud risk, requiring robust internal controls and vigilant monitoring by all bank personnel. The procedures detailed herein apply to all commercial banking divisions, wire transfer operations, and client relationship managers operating under the Cortex Bank and Trust charter.

Personnel must remain continuously alert to the primary typologies associated with Business Email Compromise. These typologies typically manifest as executive impersonation, where a threat actor masquerades as a senior corporate officer instructing a subordinate to execute an urgent, confidential wire transfer. Another prevalent scheme involves vendor invoice manipulation, wherein fraudsters compromise a supplier account to request that Cortex Bank and Trust clients redirect scheduled payments to a newly established, fraudulent account. Personnel must scrutinize communications for red flags such as manufactured urgency, sudden requests for secrecy, usage of personal email addresses by corporate executives, and subtle alterations to familiar email domains.

To mitigate these risks, Cortex Bank and Trust mandates strict adherence to out-of-band verification procedures and dual-approval controls for commercial transactions. Any client-initiated wire transfer request exceeding the threshold of fifty thousand dollars must trigger an immediate secondary verification process. This process requires the Cortex Bank and Trust relationship manager or designated wire operations specialist to contact the authorized client representative via a verified, pre-established telephone number documented in the core banking system. Telephone numbers embedded within the email request itself must never be utilized for this verification. Furthermore, if a transaction involves an amendment to existing vendor payment instructions or a first-time wire transfer to a foreign jurisdiction, the threshold for out-of-band verification is lowered to ten thousand dollars. For corporate clients initiating bulk wire transfers exceeding two hundred thousand dollars, Cortex Bank and Trust requires multifactor authentication alongside a secondary approval from an independent user within the client organization.

Cortex Bank and Trust employs automated transaction monitoring systems calibrated to detect anomalies indicative of Business Email Compromise. The compliance department has established algorithmic alerts that trigger when a commercial account exhibits sudden deviations from historical transactional baselines. System-generated alerts will activate if a client initiates a wire transfer to a beneficiary account that has no prior relationship with the client, particularly if the beneficiary institution is located in a jurisdiction previously unassociated with the client business footprint. Additionally, the system monitors for velocity anomalies, such as multiple wire transfers just below the fifty thousand dollar verification threshold occurring within a forty-eight hour window. Compliance analysts must review these alerts daily and cross-reference them against corresponding client correspondence.

In the event that a Cortex Bank and Trust employee suspects an active Business Email Compromise attempt, or if a client reports a fraudulent transfer, immediate escalation to the Fraud Investigations Unit and the Bank Secrecy Act Officer is mandatory. Wire transfer operations must immediately attempt to halt outgoing funds or, if the funds have already been transmitted, issue a recall message to the receiving institution citing suspected fraud. The account in question must be placed on a temporary debit restriction to prevent further unauthorized disbursements. The Bank Secrecy Act Officer will evaluate the incident to determine if a Suspicious Activity Report must be filed. According to Cortex Bank and Trust policy, any incident resulting in a potential loss or attempted loss exceeding five thousand dollars will necessitate the filing of a Suspicious Activity Report within thirty days of initial detection.

All Cortex Bank and Trust personnel involved in commercial banking, wire processing, and customer service must undergo specialized training on an annual basis. This training emphasizes the evolving nature of cyber-enabled financial crimes and reinforces the critical importance of strict adherence to verification thresholds and out-of-band communication protocols. Safeguarding client assets against sophisticated social engineering schemes remains a foundational pillar of the Cortex Bank and Trust compliance framework.
