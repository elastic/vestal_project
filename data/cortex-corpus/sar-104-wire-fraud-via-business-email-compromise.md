**Activity Overview**
Between November 10, 2023, and November 14, 2023, Cortex Bank and Trust identified suspicious wire transfer activity totaling $315,450.00 originating from the commercial checking account of Horizon Manufacturing Solutions LLC. The transaction was rapidly identified as a textbook instance of Wire Fraud facilitated by Business Email Compromise (BEC). The client was maliciously manipulated into altering established vendor payment instructions, resulting in a substantial unauthorized diversion of corporate funds to an unrelated third party.

**Narrative**
On November 10, 2023, Horizon Manufacturing Solutions LLC ("Horizon"), a long-standing commercial client of Cortex Bank and Trust, received an electronic communication purportedly from their primary raw materials supplier, Orion Steelworks. The email urgently requested an update to their remittance instructions for an outstanding invoice amounting to $315,450.00. The correspondence originated from the email address "ar@orion-steelworks.com." Upon subsequent forensic review, it was determined that this address visually mimicked the legitimate vendor domain "ar@orionsteelworks.com" by introducing an obscure hyphen, a common tactic used to deceive unsuspecting personnel.

Relying upon these fraudulent payment instructions, Horizon’s Accounts Payable Director logged into Cortex Bank and Trust’s corporate cash management portal on November 12, 2023. The Director initiated a domestic wire transfer of $315,450.00 directed to an account held at First Continental Bank, N.A., under the beneficiary name "Vertex Global Trading Inc." Because the Director possessed elevated administrative permissions within the banking portal, the transaction bypassed Horizon's standard dual-approval threshold and was processed immediately by Cortex Bank and Trust’s automated wire systems. 

The fraudulent nature of this transfer was discovered two days later, on November 14, 2023, when the genuine accounts receivable department at Orion Steelworks contacted Horizon regarding the severely past-due payment. Horizon immediately notified Cortex Bank and Trust's corporate fraud investigations department. Cortex Bank and Trust promptly placed a complete freeze on Horizon's online banking profile to prevent any further unauthorized disbursements. Concurrently, our wire operations department initiated a formal SWIFT recall request to First Continental Bank, N.A., citing severe fraud and requesting immediate indemnification and the return of funds.

Cortex Bank and Trust's cybersecurity personnel reviewed the authentication session logs for the November 12 wire initiation. The logs definitively confirmed the session originated from Horizon's recognized corporate IP address. This corroborated the customer's claim that their internal personnel were manipulated via social engineering, rather than their Cortex Bank and Trust online banking credentials being directly compromised by an external network breach.

**Conclusion**
Cortex Bank and Trust is filing this Suspicious Activity Report because the outgoing wire transfer of $315,450.00 exhibits all the primary typologies of Business Email Compromise. The perpetrator successfully executed a targeted spoofing campaign to redirect legitimate corporate capital to a suspect beneficiary account. At the time of this filing, First Continental Bank, N.A. has managed to freeze and recover only $42,000.00 of the original transferred amount, resulting in a significant potential financial loss of $273,450.00 for the victim. Cortex Bank and Trust will continue to rigorously monitor Horizon’s accounts for anomalous activity. The client has been advised to file a complaint with the FBI’s Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) and engage independent cybersecurity professionals to thoroughly audit their internal corporate communications infrastructure.
