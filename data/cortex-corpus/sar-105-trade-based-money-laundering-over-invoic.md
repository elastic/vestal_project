Cortex Bank and Trust initiated an investigation into Apex Industrial Importers LLC following automated system alerts regarding a sudden and unprecedented escalation in high-value, outgoing international wire transfers. The observed transactional behavior is highly indicative of Trade-Based Money Laundering (TBML), specifically utilizing an over-invoicing scheme to illicitly transfer excess funds to a foreign jurisdiction under the guise of legitimate international trade payments.

**Narrative**
Apex Industrial Importers LLC ("Apex") has maintained a Commercial Business Checking account with Cortex Bank and Trust since January 2023. Historically, the account exhibited routine domestic clearing activity and occasional low-value international wires to regional suppliers in Mexico, averaging approximately $40,000 in outgoing volume per month.

Beginning in early March 2026, Cortex Bank and Trust’s transaction monitoring systems flagged a drastic shift in Apex's account behavior. Between March 1, 2026, and July 15, 2026, Apex originated twelve (12) outgoing international wire transfers totaling exactly $3,450,200.00. These funds were exclusively remitted to a single, newly established counterparty named Helios Trading Ltd., located in Hong Kong. The wire remittance details consistently referenced vague payment purposes, such as "Electronic components - Commercial Grade."

Pursuant to Cortex Bank and Trust’s Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) protocols, the AML Investigations unit requested underlying trade documentation from the principal signer, Mr. Marcus Vance, to substantiate the significant economic shift. Mr. Vance promptly provided commercial invoices, packing lists, and bills of lading. 

A meticulous review of the provided documentation revealed severe pricing discrepancies indicative of over-invoicing. The commercial invoices from Helios Trading Ltd. billed Apex for standard 12-volt industrial cooling fans and basic ceramic capacitors. However, the unit price for the cooling fans was listed at $875.00 per unit. Independent open-source market research conducted by Cortex Bank and Trust investigators determined that the fair market value for these specific, non-specialized cooling fans ranges between $35.00 and $45.00 per unit. 

Furthermore, the customs documentation and bills of lading indicated shipping weights and container volumes that were grossly disproportionate to the stated multi-million-dollar valuation of the goods. The extreme inflation of the unit costs—amounting to an approximate 2,000% markup—suggests that the trade transaction is a structural facade designed to justify the outward remittance of substantial capital. When queried about the exorbitant pricing, Mr. Vance provided elusive explanations, citing "proprietary manufacturing techniques" and "expedited global logistics fees," which do not align with the standard, low-tech commodities listed on the customs documentation.

**Conclusion**
Cortex Bank and Trust is filing this Suspicious Activity Report due to the overwhelming presence of Trade-Based Money Laundering red flags, primarily the egregious over-invoicing of imported goods. The severe discrepancy between the invoiced prices and the true market value strongly indicates an attempt to obscure the illicit movement of funds across international borders, utilizing trade as a vehicle for capital flight. Due to the high-risk nature of these findings, Cortex Bank and Trust has restricted the account ending in 0092 to prevent further outbound international transfers and is currently presenting the entity to the risk committee for formal relationship termination.
