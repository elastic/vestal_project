**Narrative**
Cortex Bank and Trust (MTB) is filing this Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) on Marcus T. Vance and his business entity, Osprey Holdings LLC, due to a pattern of rapid pass-through wire activity culminating in a high-value, all-cash real estate purchase. This activity is highly indicative of the integration phase of money laundering, wherein illicit or unexplained funds are legitimized through the acquisition of physical, high-value assets.

Osprey Holdings LLC established its business checking account with MTB in May 2023. At the time of onboarding, Vance stated the business provided real estate consulting services, with expected monthly wire activity not exceeding $50,000. For the first five months, the account operated well within these parameters, receiving only modest domestic wires from local property management firms.

However, beginning October 5, 2023, the account’s transactional profile shifted dramatically. Over a span of five weeks, MTB observed five incoming wire transfers totaling $850,000. These funds originated from three newly formed, unrelated limited liability companies based in Delaware and Wyoming—jurisdictions known for corporate anonymity. The originators had no prior transactional history with Osprey Holdings LLC, and the transfer memos contained vague descriptions such as "Project Funding" and "Capital Injection." 

The funds were held in the MTB account for just a few days, exhibiting classic pass-through behavior. On November 12, 2023, Vance initiated an outgoing domestic wire transfer in the amount of $842,500 to Horizon Title & Escrow Company. The payment details referenced "Final Cash Closing - 4450 Oceanview Drive." 

MTB’s Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) team contacted Vance to request supporting documentation regarding the sudden influx of third-party funds and the subsequent real estate transaction. Vance provided a standard purchase agreement for a luxury residential property but could not produce formal loan documents, confirming this was a complete cash transaction without traditional mortgage financing. When questioned about the origin of the $850,000, Vance described the remitting LLCs as "silent private investors." However, he refused to provide investment agreements or identify the ultimate beneficial owners of those funding entities.

**Conclusion**
MTB deems this activity highly suspicious as it aligns with established typologies for real estate-based money laundering. The subject utilized the MTB account to consolidate large, unexplained funds from opaque third-party corporate entities before immediately integrating those funds into the legitimate economy via an all-cash property acquisition. The lack of standard financing, combined with the subject's refusal to substantiate the source of the incoming funds, poses a significant regulatory risk. In response, Cortex Bank and Trust has elevated Osprey Holdings LLC’s risk rating to severe, restricted outgoing wire capabilities on the account, and is currently reviewing the relationship for immediate closure.
