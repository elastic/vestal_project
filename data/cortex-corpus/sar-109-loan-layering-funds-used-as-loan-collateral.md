**Activity Overview**
Cortex Bank and Trust is filing this Suspicious Activity Report regarding Julian Vance and his business entity, Vance Consulting Group LLC. Between March 1, 2026, and July 15, 2026, the Subject engaged in a series of transactions indicative of loan layering and the use of unverified funds as loan collateral. The total aggregate amount of suspicious activity is $810,000. The activity is characterized by the receipt of high-value international wires, the immediate placement of these funds into a cash-collateralized Certificate of Deposit (CD), and the subsequent drawdown of a secured line of credit, lacking an apparent legitimate business or economic purpose.

**Detailed Narrative**
On March 1, 2026, Julian Vance opened a Business Checking account on behalf of Vance Consulting Group LLC, stating the business provided corporate logistics consulting. Customer Due Diligence (CDD) profile expectations indicated anticipated monthly domestic ACH and wire activity of approximately $50,000. 

Between March 15 and April 20, 2026, the Business Checking account received four incoming international wire transfers totaling $450,000 from an entity identified as "Apex Global Holdings," located in Dubai, UAE. The wire details listed vague references such as "Consulting Retainer" and "Q2 Contract Services." 

On April 25, 2026, Vance transferred $400,000 of these funds to a newly opened 12-month Certificate of Deposit (CD) at Cortex Bank and Trust. Shortly thereafter, on May 5, 2026, Vance applied for and was granted a $360,000 commercial line of credit (LOC), utilizing the newly funded $400,000 CD as 100% cash collateral. 

The layering phase of the suspected laundering cycle commenced on May 10, 2026. Over the next six weeks, Vance drew down the entire $360,000 LOC in four separate tranches. Instead of utilizing the funds for standard business operating expenses as outlined in his KYC profile, Vance immediately wired the LOC proceeds to third parties. Notably, $250,000 was wired to an escrow account belonging to a luxury real estate title company in Miami, Florida, and $110,000 was wired to a high-end exotic automotive dealership in Texas. 

The activity appears highly suspicious as it makes no economic sense. By borrowing against his own newly deposited funds, Vance incurred an interest rate spread of 4.5% on the LOC, effectively paying a premium to access his own liquidity. This methodology is a known money laundering typology used to integrate illicit funds into the financial system by disguising them as legitimate loan proceeds, making the funds appear clean to the receiving real estate and automotive vendors. Furthermore, on July 10, 2026, Vance requested to prematurely break the CD—incurring early withdrawal penalties—utilizing the collateral to pay off the $360,000 LOC principal and requesting the remaining balance via a cashier’s check made payable to himself.

**Conclusion/Action**
Cortex Bank and Trust’s AML Investigations unit determined that the transaction pattern serves no logical business purpose and appears designed solely to obscure the origin of funds received from a high-risk jurisdiction. Consequently, Cortex Bank and Trust has frozen the remaining funds and initiated the account closure process for Vance Consulting Group LLC, effective August 1, 2026. Due to the rapid extraction of funds into high-value luxury assets and the clear demonstration of loan layering, a direct referral of this case has been forwarded to the local field office of the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) Financial Crimes Task Force. Cortex Bank and Trust will continue to monitor and restrict any residual activity during the final account closure and offboarding period.
