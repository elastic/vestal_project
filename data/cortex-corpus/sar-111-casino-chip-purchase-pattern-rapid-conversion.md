**Activity Overview**
Cortex Bank and Trust’s Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Investigations Unit is filing this Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) due to a pattern of rapid movement of funds indicative of layering and integration through casino operations. Between March 1, 2026, and April 15, 2026, the Subject received multiple high-value, uncharacteristic wire transfers totaling $85,000. These funds were rapidly withdrawn at casino cages, converted into casino chips, and subsequently redeposited into the Subject’s Cortex Bank and Trust account primarily in the form of casino checks. This pattern suggests the Subject is purchasing casino chips with minimal or no corresponding gaming activity to disguise the original source of funds and legitimize them as "casino winnings."

**Detailed Narrative**
The Subject established a Personal Checking account with Cortex Bank and Trust on January 12, 2022, listing his occupation as a self-employed business consultant. Prior to the review period, the account exhibited routine activity, averaging $6,000 in monthly deposits and commensurate living expenses. 

The suspicious activity commenced on March 5, 2026, when the Subject received a domestic wire transfer of $45,000 from an unfamiliar corporate entity, "Apex Global Logistics LLC." The following day, March 6, the Subject initiated two Point-of-Sale (POS) cash advances totaling $40,000 at the main cage of the Solstice Grand Resort & Casino in Las Vegas. On March 8, less than 48 hours later, the Subject deposited a casino check issued by the Solstice Grand Resort & Casino in the amount of $39,200 via mobile deposit. 

A similar sequence occurred later in the month. On March 20, 2026, a second wire transfer of $40,000 posted to the account from the same originator, Apex Global Logistics LLC. Between March 21 and March 22, the Subject conducted $42,000 in cash advances at the Sapphire Plaza Casino. On March 25, the Subject visited a Cortex Bank and Trust branch to deposit $15,000 in physical cash alongside another casino check issued by the Sapphire Plaza Casino for $25,500. 

The investigative review highlights several severe red flags. The rapid conversion cycle (withdrawing funds at a casino and redepositing them within two to four days) is highly inconsistent with legitimate recreational gambling. The nominal difference between the amounts withdrawn ($82,000) and the amounts subsequently redeposited ($79,700) represents a loss of only 2.8%. This marginal discrepancy strongly suggests the Subject is exchanging funds for chips, holding them briefly, and cashing them out without engaging in significant gaming play. The issuance of casino checks effectively cleans the funds, creating a false paper trail of gambling revenue. Furthermore, Cortex Bank and Trust issued a Request for Information (RFI) to the Subject regarding the incoming wires from Apex Global Logistics LLC; the Subject provided vague, non-itemized invoices for "strategic advising" that did not substantiate the transaction volume.

**Conclusion/Action**
Due to the unverified source of the incoming wires and the textbook rapid conversion of funds through casino chip purchases to obscure the audit trail, Cortex Bank and Trust deems this activity highly suspicious and indicative of money laundering. As a result of this investigation, Cortex Bank and Trust has restricted the Subject’s account and initiated the 30-day account closure process in accordance with internal risk policies. This case has been formally referred to the Nevada High Intensity Drug Trafficking Area (HIDTA) Financial Task Force and the FBI Las Vegas Field Office for further criminal investigation.
