**Subject Information**
Subject Name: Bancorp of the Antilles (BotA)
Account Type: USD Foreign Correspondent Account
Account Number: 4409-8821-3390
Subject Location: Willemstad, Curaçao
Review Period: May 1, 2026, through July 31, 2026

**Activity Overview**
Cortex Bank and Trust (MTB) is filing this Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) on its respondent bank customer, Bancorp of the Antilles (BotA), due to suspicious wire transfer activity indicative of unauthorized nested correspondent banking. During the review period, MTB identified 2,835 wire transfers totaling approximately $90.4 million ($45.2M incoming / $45.2M outgoing) passing through BotA’s USD Correspondent Account. The transaction patterns, volume, and originator information strongly suggest BotA is providing downstream correspondent clearing services to undisclosed third-party financial institutions and Money Services Businesses (MSBs) located in high-risk jurisdictions.

**Detailed Narrative**
Bancorp of the Antilles established a USD Correspondent Account with Cortex Bank and Trust in March 2022. According to the institution’s USA PATRIOT Act Certification and initial Customer Due Diligence (CDD) profile, the account was intended to facilitate international trade payments for BotA’s direct retail and commercial clientele in the Caribbean region. Anticipated monthly volume was historically projected and observed at approximately $10 million.

In June 2026, MTB’s automated AML transaction monitoring system generated multiple high-velocity alerts on the BotA account. An investigation by the MTB Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) revealed a 350% deviation from historical baselines. Between May 1, 2026, and July 31, 2026, the account processed 1,420 incoming wires totaling $45,210,500 and 1,415 outgoing wires totaling $45,190,000. The activity functioned as a rapid pass-through, with funds entering and exiting the account on the same business day, leaving a nominal end-of-day balance.

A detailed manual review of SWIFT MT103 and MT202 payment messages revealed that the originating entities were not direct commercial customers of BotA. In over 80% of the alerted transactions, the "Ordering Institution" or "Ordering Customer" fields (Field 52a/50a) identified third-party financial entities, primarily *Vanguard Exchange House* (based in Dubai, UAE) and *Pacific Remit Ltd* (based in Panama). 

The funds originated from these nested intermediaries and were subsequently cleared through Cortex Bank and Trust to various shell-like limited liability companies in Eastern Europe and Southeast Asia. The payment references frequently lacked clear economic purpose, citing generic terms such as "Consulting Services," "Software Licensing," and "Invoice 8839." 

On July 12, 2026, Cortex Bank and Trust issued a formal Request for Information (RFI) to BotA’s compliance department, requesting enhanced due diligence documentation on the originators and the commercial purpose of the anomalous wires. BotA responded on July 20, 2026, stating that the originators were "indirect affiliates utilizing our gateway infrastructure." BotA failed to provide independent KYC documentation for the nested entities, explicitly violating MTB’s strict policy against downstream correspondent banking (nesting) as outlined in the Master Correspondent Banking Agreement.

**Conclusion/Action**
Cortex Bank and Trust considers this activity highly suspicious due to the deceptive use of the correspondent account to facilitate high-volume, pass-through transactions for undisclosed, high-risk nested intermediaries. The obfuscation of the true originators presents a significant money laundering and potential sanctions evasion risk, completely outside of MTB's risk appetite.

Consequently, Cortex Bank and Trust has escalated this matter to the Enterprise Risk Committee, which voted to exit the relationship with Bancorp of the Antilles. A 30-day notice of account closure was issued on August 1, 2026, and the account has been dynamically restricted to process only returning funds and pre-approved stranded transactions. Furthermore, due to the high-risk jurisdictions involved and the volume of suspicious funds, MTB’s AML Officer has proactively referred this case and the underlying SWIFT data to the Homeland Security Investigations (HSI) Illicit Finance Division. The internal investigation is now considered resolved pending the final legal closure of the account on August 31, 2026.
