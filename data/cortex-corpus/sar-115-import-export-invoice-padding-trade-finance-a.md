**Activity Overview**
This Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) is being filed by Cortex Bank and Trust to report suspected Trade-Based Money Laundering (TBML), specifically import/export invoice padding and value transfer. Between February 1, 2026, and May 31, 2026, the Subject account initiated 14 outgoing international wire transfers totaling $3,450,000 to a single foreign supplier. A review of the underlying trade documentation by the Bank's compliance department revealed gross over-valuation of imported goods, misrepresentation of shipping weights, and rapid pass-through fund movements indicative of illicit capital flight rather than legitimate international commerce.

**Detailed Narrative**
Cortex Bank and Trust's automated AML transaction monitoring system generated an alert on Global Horizon Traders LLC on April 15, 2026, following a sudden 400% increase in outgoing international wire volume compared to historical account averages. The Subject’s Know Your Customer (KYC) profile indicates they operate as a mid-sized wholesale distributor of standard consumer electronic parts. 

During the review period, the Subject received approximately $3.5 million in domestic wire transfers from several unverified domestic shell entities listing their business types as "logistics consulting." Typically within 24 to 48 hours of receiving these funds, the Subject originated large, often even-dollar international wires to a counterparty identified as "Apex Global Components FZE," located in a free trade zone in Dubai, United Arab Emirates. 

To substantiate these high-value transfers, Cortex Bank and Trust’s Trade Finance Compliance unit issued a Request for Information (RFI) to obtain commercial invoices and Bills of Lading (BOLs) from the Subject. Analysis of the provided documentation revealed severe pricing discrepancies consistent with invoice padding. For example, a March 18, 2026 invoice supported a $680,000 outgoing wire for a shipment of 20,000 "Standard 12V DC Relays." The invoiced unit price was $34.00 per relay. Open-source market analysis indicates the fair market value for this specific commercial component is approximately $1.50 to $2.00 per unit, representing an artificial markup of nearly 2,000%.

Furthermore, the corresponding BOL for an April 10, 2026 shipment (Invoice #4409 supporting an $850,000 wire) indicated a total shipping weight of 1,200 kilograms. The commercial invoice claimed the shipment contained dense bulk copper wiring and heavy-duty industrial transformers. The declared weight on the BOL is physically impossible for the volume and type of goods invoiced, strongly suggesting phantom shipments or severe under-shipment utilized to disguise international value transfer.

Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) conducted on the counterparty, Apex Global Components FZE, revealed no legitimate digital footprint, no dedicated commercial website, and a registered address shared with over 200 other entities managed by a known corporate formation agency.

**Conclusion/Action**
Due to the egregious nature of the invoice padding, the lack of economic purpose for the extreme markups, the use of a high-risk free trade zone, and the pass-through nature of the account activity, Cortex Bank and Trust assesses this activity as highly suspicious and indicative of TBML. 

Cortex Bank and Trust is currently in the process of exiting the relationship with Global Horizon Traders LLC. A 30-day account closure notice was issued on June 5, 2026, and all international wire privileges have been suspended. Given the cross-border nature of the suspected illicit value transfer, Cortex Bank and Trust’s AML Investigations unit has proactively referred this matter to Homeland Security Investigations (HSI) - Miami Trade Transparency Unit for further criminal review. This investigation is considered closed by the Bank pending final account offboarding and the dispersal of the remaining $14,203.55 balance via cashier's check.
