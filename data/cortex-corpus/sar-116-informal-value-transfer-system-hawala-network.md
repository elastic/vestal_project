**ACTIVITY OVERVIEW**
Cortex Bank and Trust is filing this Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) to document activity indicative of an unlicensed Money Services Business (MSB) and an Informal Value Transfer System (IVTS), specifically resembling a hawala network. Between February 1, 2023, and July 31, 2023, the subject account exhibited a rapid flow-through of funds totaling $412,850.00 in aggregated credits and $409,500.00 in aggregated debits. The transactional behavior deviates entirely from the stated business purpose and features anomalous third-party funding followed by immediate offshore wire transfers. 

**DETAILED NARRATIVE**
This investigation was initiated following an automated transaction monitoring alert on May 12, 2023, regarding a sudden spike in international wire velocity. A historical review of Crescent Horizon General Trading LLC’s account revealed a sustained pattern of pass-through activity.

During the review period, the account received 214 incoming credits totaling $412,850.00. These inflows consisted predominantly of third-party peer-to-peer (P2P) transfers via Zelle ($145,300.00), incoming domestic ACH transfers ($182,550.00), and cash deposits made at multiple Cortex Bank and Trust branch ATMs ($85,000.00). The incoming electronic transfers originated from over 60 distinct individuals across the United States. Many of the P2P transfer memos contained notations inconsistent with the purchase of wholesale goods, such as "family support," "Eid gift," "for Karachi," and "uncle medical."

The cash deposits were frequently structured just below reporting thresholds, typically ranging from $2,000.00 to $8,500.00, and were deposited by unidentified individuals at various ATMs outside the subject’s primary geographic footprint. 

Once the account balance accrued between $30,000.00 and $50,000.00, Mr. Mahmood executed large, rounded-dollar outgoing wire transfers. Over the six-month period, the subject initiated 11 outgoing international wires totaling $409,500.00. All wires were directed to three distinct corporate beneficiaries located in Dubai, United Arab Emirates (UAE), and Istanbul, Turkey. The beneficiary names (e.g., "Apex Global Logistics FZE") suggest general trading firms, a common conduit used to settle hawala ledgers via trade-based money laundering (TBML) mechanisms. Funds rarely remained in the Cortex Bank and Trust account for more than 48 hours before being wired overseas.

Furthermore, a review of the account debit profile revealed a complete absence of normal operational expenses expected of a wholesale import/export business. There were no payments for payroll, warehouse leasing, domestic shipping, logistics, or utilities. 

The aggregation of small-dollar, consumer-to-business electronic payments and cash deposits from unrelated individuals, followed by bulk outward remittances to high-risk jurisdictions to settle presumed trade invoices, is highly characteristic of an informal value transfer system. Cortex Bank and Trust assesses that Mr. Mahmood is likely operating as an unlicensed hawaladar, collecting fiat currency from local diaspora members to facilitate corresponding payouts in their home countries.

**CONCLUSION / ACTION**
Due to the highly suspicious nature of the activity, the lack of transparency regarding the ultimate originators and beneficiaries of the funds, and the operation of an apparent unlicensed MSB/hawala network, Cortex Bank and Trust has elected to exit the relationship. The subject’s account was frozen on August 5, 2023, and officially closed on August 15, 2023, with a cashier's check for the remaining balance of $3,350.00 mailed to the address on file. Additionally, due to the potential violation of 18 U.S.C. § 1960 (Unlicensed Money Remitting Businesses), Cortex Bank and Trust’s AML Investigations Unit has formally referred this matter to the regional Homeland Security Investigations (HSI) El Dorado Task Force. The investigation is now considered closed by the Bank, pending further law enforcement inquiries.
