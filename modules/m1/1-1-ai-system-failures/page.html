Lab 1.1: Diagnose Tina's failures

Tina is Cortex Bank's AI compliance assistant. This morning she sent three responses to compliance questions, and all three were wrong in different ways. In this track you diagnose her failures and repair the system prompt that caused them.

The problem

Two of the three responses Build 1 opens with, as the notebook prints them:

--- response_1 ---
{
  "id": "response_1",
  "text": "The CTR threshold is $5,000 for cash
           transactions. A $9,500 deposit would
           be reportable."
}

--- response_3 ---
{
  "id": "response_3",
  "answer": "The SAR filing deadline is 45 days
             from the triggering transaction.",
  "policy_id": "policy-011",
  "confidence": "high"
}

Tina gave both with confidence. Cortex Bank's CTR threshold is $10,000 (policy-003), and policy-011 sets the SAR deadline at 30 days. Cortex files the wrong reports if either ships. A third response, not shown here (response_2), is not an answer at all. Each one is wrong in a different way, and Build 1 asks you to name how.

Three model types, three jobs

     

An LLM generates text. An embedding model positions text in a vector space. A reranker scores and orders candidate documents. Each type has one job. Giving it the wrong job produces a distinct failure pattern.

When the wrong model gets the job

User: What is the CTR threshold?

Response from embedding endpoint:
[0.023, -0.451, 0.887, 0.112,
 -0.334, 0.556, 0.091, ...]
(1,024 floats, not an answer)

Ask an embedding model to "answer" a question and it returns a vector. The UI may show nothing, or raw numbers. There is no error, only the wrong output type for the task.

Hallucination is confident

Tina: The threshold is $5,000.
      A $9,500 deposit is reportable.

policy-003 (actual text):
"...any single transaction
 involving physical currency
 that equals or exceeds ten
 thousand dollars."

The $5,000 answer cites no source and gives no sign that it is wrong. Same format, same confidence, wrong value: a hallucination. The only way to catch it is to compare it with the authoritative source.

Schema-valid is not correct

{
  "answer":
    "SAR filing deadline: 45 days",
  "policy_id": "policy-011",
  "confidence": "high"
}

policy-011 (actual text):
"...electronically transmitted
 within thirty calendar days."

The output has the right shape and even cites policy-011. But policy-011 says 30 days, not 45. An answer whose cited source does not state its value is ungrounded: a hallucination behind a citation. Schema validation passes it; semantic validation catches it. Lab 1.2 builds that validator.

Anatomy of a system prompt

A well-structured prompt has four regions: Role (who Tina is), Schema (the required output format), Constraints (rules to follow) and the Grounding instruction (cite retrieved text). Remove any one and Tina's behavior changes.

How you will prove a fix

# Ablation: remove each element,
# re-run 6 questions,
# record how many stay grounded.

Element removed | Grounded
role            | ? / 6
schema          | ? / 6
constraints     | ? / 6
grounding       | ? / 6  <- your results
                           will differ

The check runs your prompt against 6 held-out questions, then runs it again with each prompt element removed. The element whose removal drops the grounded count most is the decisive one. Your ablation results decide the Defend answer.

Decision rule

Take the first row that applies. Your ablation results answer it, not general knowledge.

Your ablationQuestion 1 answer
No removal lowered the grounded countNo single element
One section's removal caused the largest drop in grounded answersThat section
Two or more sections tie for the largest dropAny one of them

What done looks like

  • Endpoint types run: all 3 (LLM, embedding model, reranker)
  • Diagnoses correct: 3 of 3
  • Held-out responses schema-valid: 6 of 6
  • Held-out responses grounded: at least 5 of 6
