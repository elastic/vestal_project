Lab 2.2: Tune retrieval quality

The corpus is indexed. Now you choose how Tina searches it. Four retriever strategies, two quality metrics, one production decision.

Tip: Select Hide Instructions in the top bar to give the Brief full width.

The problem

An analyst asks Tina for WF-2026-0803A, from wire-fraud-202's case number, MTB-WF-2026-0803A. Tina's only retriever searches by meaning:

by meaning (Tina today)   168 ms
 1  wire-fraud-204
 2  wire-fraud-207
 3  wire-fraud-202   the case asked for
 4  sar-111

by exact words             54 ms
 1  wire-fraud-202

A case number has no meaning to embed, so two cases that read alike rank above the one asked for. Exact words find it first, in a third of the time. You run this search yourself at the start of Build 1.

Four strategies

StrategyStrength
BM25Exact keyword match: wins on identifier queries
Dense semanticMeaning over keywords
Hybrid (RRF)Hedges between the two; it can trail the stronger leg on your corpus
Hybrid + rerankOften the strongest ranking, but adds latency; measure it on your corpus

Measuring a ranking

Each metric scores the top k results of one question, then averages over the question set. Worked example: a question with 3 relevant documents, and a top 5 with relevant hits at ranks 1 and 3.

MetricWhat it measuresExample
precision@kRelevant results in the top k, divided by k2 / 5 = 0.40
recall@kRelevant results in the top k, divided by all relevant documents2 / 3 = 0.67
MRR1 / the rank of the first relevant result1 / 1 = 1.00
nDCG@kRelevant results weighted toward the top, scaled so the ideal order scores 1Higher when the hit at rank 3 moves to rank 2

This lab grades nDCG@5, and MRR on identifier questions, where one exact document is the answer.

Sizing the vector index

On Serverless, a new semantic_text index with dense embeddings is already quantized with Better Binary Quantization (bbq_hnsw), at the default m of 16. Tuning changes the graph, m and ef_construction, not the quantization.

Per vectorBytesAt 1,024 dimensions, m = 16
BBQ vector⌈dims / 64⌉ × 8 + 14142
HNSW graph4 × m64

A larger m gives each vector more graph neighbors: better recall at production scale, and more memory.

Decision rule

Your own measurements decide each row.

DecisionRule
Default retrieverThe highest nDCG@5 among templates whose p50 fits the budget
Exact lookupsThe template with the highest identifier MRR
Graph memory4 × m bytes per vector, times the vector count

What done looks like

  • All four templates return documents for every question, each with nDCG@5 at least 0.4
  • bm25 identifier MRR at least 0.90
  • hybrid_rerank nDCG@5 target 0.75, reported, not graded
  • cortex-corpus-tuned: bbq_hnsw at the largest m that fits your budget, ef_construction at least 100, same document count as cortex-corpus
  • Hybrid nDCG@5 moves by at most 0.03 after tuning
