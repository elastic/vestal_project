Lab 2.3: Embedding lifecycle

A new embedding model is available. Before it ships, you must benchmark it, cut over without downtime, and raise recall for the queries it still gets wrong.

Tip: Select Hide Instructions in the top bar to give the Brief full width.

The problem

An analyst asks Tina, in their own words:

query:     money moved around to hide where it came from
target:    sar-103  (shell company layering and ownership obfuscation)
retrieved: sar-109, sar-105, sar-116, sar-110

Based on the documents provided, several cases describe money being moved
around to hide its origin:

**Loan Layering** [sar-109]: Vance deposited funds into a CD, then borrowed
against them via a Line of Credit and immediately wired the proceeds to
third parties ...

The case that answers it, sar-103, is not in Tina's top 5. The query has none of its words: layering, shell company, ownership. Keyword search needs them, and the current model's semantic match ranks other cases first. A new embedding model is ready to test, and cortex-corpus-live must stay searchable while you move to it.

Benchmark before you switch

A newer model is a hypothesis until it is measured on queries shaped like Tina's traffic. Two numbers decide it.

MeasureWhat it tells you
nDCG@5Ranking quality from 0 to 1: the labelled document near the top of the first 5 results scores higher
GainCandidate nDCG@5 minus the current model's. A gain of 0.02 or less is noise, not an improvement
p95 latencyThe time within which 95% of searches finish. It must fit the latency budget

Swap the alias in one request

Elasticsearch applies every action in one _aliases request together. Two requests, a remove and then an add, leave a moment with no index behind the alias, and every search in that moment fails.

POST _aliases
{"actions": [
  {"remove": {"index": "cortex-corpus",           "alias": "cortex-corpus-live"}},
  {"add":    {"index": "cortex-corpus-candidate", "alias": "cortex-corpus-live"}}
]}

The old index stays, so a rollback is the same request reversed.

Raise recall from the query side

Some queries miss because they share no words with the document that answers them. You can fix that without touching the index, by changing what you search with.

TechniqueWhat you search with
Query rewritingThe analyst's question restated in the corpus's own terms, or several variants of it
HyDE (hypothetical document)A passage a model writes as if it answered the question

Both need a model call: cortex-generation, the completion endpoint you pinned in lab 2.1, or the same models through tina.client.llm_client().

Decision rule

Gain is the candidate's nDCG@5 minus the current model's. A gain of 0.02 or less, including a negative one, is noise.

Gain over currentCandidate p95Call
More than 0.02Within budgetKeep the cutover
0.02 or lessWithin budgetRoll back: no real gain
More than 0.02Over budgetRoll back: too slow
0.02 or lessOver budgetRoll back: both

What done looks like

  • Benchmark set: at least 12 labelled queries, covering policy, sar and wire-fraud
  • cortex-corpus-candidate embeds with the candidate endpoint
  • cortex-corpus-live points only to cortex-corpus-candidate; cortex-corpus is kept
  • Probe during the move: at least 20 samples, 0 failed and 0 empty searches
  • Live index mapping and document count unchanged
  • rewrite(): at least 8 of 13 held-out queries keyword search misses find their target in the first 5 documents, all calls within 35 seconds
  • Defend: gains of 0.02 or less are noise; your p95 budget is 300 or 500 ms
